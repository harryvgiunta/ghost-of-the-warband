# Ironwood Archer

Work from the repository root containing `project.godot`. Paths below are repository-relative, not relative to this skill directory. Treat the current code and the user's requested behavior as authoritative; this skill records the existing contracts, not a prohibition on intentional changes.

## Orient before editing

1. Read the relevant portions of `README.md` and locate the affected symbols and callers. Preserve existing GDScript, signal, and scene conventions.
2. Separate shared gameplay from controller-specific input. A frontier-only fix is incomplete when the same feature is available in the arena.
3. Inspect the current launcher and setup files before changing the engine version or executable paths. Use the managed engine or the user's `GODOT_PATH`; do not introduce a new toolchain.
4. Check whether a GDScript language server is available before symbol navigation/refactors. If unavailable, use scoped symbol searches and Godot's parser/runtime; do not claim LSP verification.

## Gameplay contracts to preserve

### Main menu, town and in-world state

- `project.godot` sets `run/main_scene="res://title.tscn"`; every normal launch opens the **main menu**: **Start New Game**, **Load Saved Game**, **Host Open World**, **Join Game**. `Play.cmd` therefore opens the title screen. `Host World.cmd` launches `main.tscn` with `--world-host --world-port 7777 --world-name "%USERNAME%"`; `Arena.cmd` opens `arena.tscn` directly for the standalone lobby. `menu.tscn`, `menu.gd`, `menu_hud.gd` and `field_inventory.gd` are deleted; do not reintroduce an out-of-game depot.
- **Start New Game** calls `PlayerState.reset(true)` and loads `main.tscn`; the ranger spawns at `world.player_spawn == town_entry`, the south gate `(0,3,47)`, facing into Hearthstead. **Load Saved Game** calls `PlayerState.load_state()` and also loads `main.tscn`; it is disabled when `state.save_path` does not exist. **Host Open World** arms `Session.arm_world("world-host",...)` on the remembered/`--world-port` port, remembers `NetCache.mode == "world"`, prints `IRONWOOD_HOST_WORLD port=`, and loads `main.tscn` **without** resetting or reloading `PlayerState`. **Join Game** validates name/address/port, offers **Arena match**, **Delve party** or **Open world**, remembers name/address/port/mode in `NetCache`, arms `Session.arm_arena("client",...)`, `Session.arm_delve("delve-join",...)` or `Session.arm_world("world-join",...)`, and loads the matching scene.
- Gallowgarth is a **second settlement, not the town**: `range_world._build_warden_village` builds it at `VILLAGE (58, 22)` in the north-eastern woods, reached by `VILLAGE_PATH` (the second footpath, off the ruin road at `(12,-10)`). It uses `VillageKit`, not `CampKit`: a 15 m `palisade` with gaps at the entry and back, the entry `gate`, three `keeper_hut` shells (their 7 x 6 m collider contract and back doorway are reused as dwellings, so no keeper station exists there), three `wattle_hut` rounds, three `totem`s, `great_pit` at the centre, `sacrifice_ground`, `effigy`, a `gallows_tree` in the doodad batch, `trader_frame`, two `drying_rack`s, two `woodpile`s, and the small dressing (chopping block, pots, bone heap, cauldron, skull stakes, four `add_figurine`, three `add_hanging`). It adds no interactables, no vendors and no stash; `interactables` stays at ten. `generation_stats` reports `village_stakes`, `village_huts`, `village_round_huts` and `village_hangers`, and the village is its own map landmark and HUD region. `palisade` and `wattle_hut` colliders take the ring angle as their yaw (a box's local X runs tangent); passing a perpendicular instead leaves walk-through gaps in the fence.
- The town is built by `range_world.gd._build_village`: the Watchfall fortress and the Suncrest shrine no longer exist - their builders, their colliders and their tree/grass clearings were deleted, and the three waystones (`-55,-25`, `55,-60`, `-15,-100`) still stand in the open with their wolf guards. **Smith Brann** in `ForesterHouse` (`vendor` 1), **Storekeeper Orla** in `FletcherWorkshop` (`vendor` 0), **Quartermaster Vey** in `HearthsteadStore` (`vendor` 2), the **Town Stash** chest under the market awning, the **Arena Portal** at `(7,3,44)`, the **campfire**, and the **delve hatch**. Each keeper has a counter and a placeholder humanoid, and its interactable point is on the customer side. `main.gd` dispatches `kind == "vendor"`/`"stash"`/`"portal"`/`"camp"`/`"waystone"`; the `kind == "delve"` entry is a map marker only and must be skipped by the prompt loop.
- The Unholy Forest camp is **not** the town and must stay out of it: `range_world._build_forest_camp` builds six sapling-and-brush shelters (1.9-2.9 m wide, 1.2-1.6 m tall) in clusters round a small fire at `CAMPSITE (-100, -22)` - three dens west, two south, one hunter's den out at `(-111, -30)` - ~110 m into the deep western stand and reachable only by the wardens' footpath `TRAIL` (kept out of `_routes` so it gets its own 2 m dirt, a 2.4-8 m soft flattening, trees closing to 3.1 m and a narrow clear in `_grass_reserved`). `_build_trail_marks` staggers eight marks along the path (bone arches, clootie trees, mask stakes, skull rings, a charm web, straw figures). The main hand decides bow / sword / dagger / staff / unarmed; the off hand decides shield / focus / second one-hander. ### Equipment slots and item taxonomy

- Armor is packed three bits per slot for five sets. `ArmorData.PIECE_MASK` (15 bits) and `ArmorData.SHIELD_BIT` (bit 15) are one contract shared by `archer.gd`, `arena_actor.gd`, `arena_hud.gd`, `delve.gd`, `seat_loadout.gd`, `main.gd` and the snapshots; never hardcode `1023` or `1024`, and move both constants together if a sixth set ever appears. Makeshift and Ranger leather are real 20%/10% sets now (head/body); mail is 30%/25% and plate 50%/30%. A slot with no piece packs the all-ones code (`ArmorData.NONE`) and guards nothing on either channel.
- `EquipmentRules.SLOTS` is `["head","chest","hands","legs","feet","neck","ring1","ring2","main","off"]`; `SLOT_LABELS` names them Head/Chest/Hands/Legs/Boots/Neck/Ring 1/Ring 2/Main Hand/Off-Hand for the HUD. Keep this order for the paperdoll, `PlayerState.equipped` and `build_loadout`; do not reintroduce a backpack-resident equipped kit.
- Item categories are `1h`, `2h`, `bow`, `staff`, `shield`, `focus`, `armor`, `jewelry` and the migration-only `armor_bundle`. `EquipmentRules.allowed(item, slot)` is the single legality rule: one-handers fit main or off; two-handers, bows and staffs fit main only; shields and foci fit off only; armor fits the slot named by its `slot` key; jewelry fits neck or either ring. `auto_slot` prefers main for one-handers, off for shields/foci, the item's armor slot, neck for necklaces, and fills ring 1 then ring 2 before replacing ring 1.
- `plan_equip` is pure and returns fresh `{ok,message,equipped,inventory,floor}` copies. It removes the source item from the backpack first so a swap can reuse that slot, then displaces any previous occupant. A two-handed/bow/staff main-hand equip displaces the old main and the old off item, clearing `off`. An off-hand equip displaces a two-handed/bow/staff main into the backpack and leaves the main empty, then displaces the old off item. Each displaced item goes to the backpack if there is room, otherwise to `floor_items`.
- `unequip_to_backpack` refuses when the backpack is full. `drop_from_inventory` and `drop_equipped` move a piece to `floor_items`; `pickup_floor` and `equip_from_inventory` require room and never drop. `try_pickup` is world loot and appends to the backpack only, never equipping. `move_equipped`/`plan_move` handles paperdoll-to-paperdoll moves and swaps, requires each piece to be legal in its destination, and refuses any result that leaves a two-handed main with an occupied off hand.
- `build_loadout` is the only bridge to a session: it maps bows to `weapon="bow"`, one/two-handed swords to `weapon="sword"`, daggers to `weapon="dagger"`, staffs to `weapon="sword"` with `sword=2` plus a staff index, an off shield to `shield=true`, an off focus to `{"kind","index"}`, and a second one-hander to `off_weapon`. `Archer.apply_loadout` is presentation only and re-checks every setter: it shows the staff instead of the greatsword while `staff_active`, shows a focus only with a supported one-handed main, sockets the off weapon, and selects the shield variant by id (buckler 0, kite 1, tower 2).
- A staff uses `Archer.STAFF_DATA` lengths (1.75 m ember, 1.60 m bone), the greatsword damage/timing and its own middle-grip reach (`STAFF_REACH` 1.6/1.5 m, mirrored in `PlayerState.ITEM_STATS`); it is two-handed and locks the shield. A focus is held and cosmetic (`focus_item.gd`), with no combat effect. The shield is legal with the dagger or any one-handed sword (`can_equip_shield`: `weapon == "dagger"` or `weapon == "sword" and sword_index <= 1`); bow, greatsword, staff and dual wield exclude it.
- The HUD is read-only: `InventorySlot` carries `{area,index,slot,item}` drag payloads and emits `right_clicked` / `slot_dropped`. Panels are `header_panel`, `purse_panel`, `paperdoll_panel`, `backpack_panel`, `stash_panel`, `vendor_panel` and `action_panel`; slot arrays are `paperdoll_buttons` (10), `backpack_buttons` (10), `stash_buttons` (20), `stock_buttons` (28), `vendor_buttons` (3) and dynamic `floor_buttons`. Right-click equips a backpack item with an empty target, or `"off"` while Alt is held; right-click on the paperdoll unequips, on the stash unstashes, and on the floor picks up. Drops route backpack→paperdoll slot, backpack→stash, backpack→floor, paperdoll→paperdoll, paperdoll→floor, stash→backpack, and floor→backpack. Dropping on the 3D view (`_on_world_dropped`) drops a backpack or paperdoll item at the ranger's feet.

### Trinkets and the ward

- `TrinketStats` owns the numbers; never re-derive a bonus in a controller. `PlayerState.trinkets()` reports `{neck,ring1,ring2,off}` and `PlayerState.trinket_stats()` the aggregate.
- Additive and multiplicative on purpose: `+25` health and `+150` stamina are flat, everything else is a fraction (`0.10` is +10%). Guard is added to whichever armor channel a hit used and capped by `TrinketStats.GUARD_CAP` (60%), above the armor-only 50% head / 30% body figures.
- The maxima are per-actor (`main.gd.max_health/max_stamina`, `ArenaActor.max_health/max_stamina`). Clamp the live pool whenever the kit changes; heal caps, consumable fullness checks, stagger fractions and respawns all read the actor's maximum, not `100`.
- The third ability slot resolves the worn grant: `TrinketStats.ability_item` prefers neck, then ring 1, then ring 2, and `AbilityRules.granted` returns `[main, off, trinket]` with `SLOT_COUNT == 3`. Input is **Shift + 3** in all three controllers; the action validators accept slot 2 unchanged.
- The **Amulet of Energy Shield** grants `energy_shield`: `absorb 0.5`, `stamina_scale 2.0`, `seconds 60`. `start_ward` stores those on the actor; `_absorb_with_ward` pays only what stamina can cover, so a dry pool lets the rest through. The bubble hides on expiry, camp return and respawn. Replicas apply `ward` from the snapshot.
- The **Ring of Dash** grants `dash`, a crosshair-aimed double-speed step; it is a copy of the unarmed Charge's numbers under its own definition so the tile shows `SHIFT + 3`.
- **Skull of Life** and **Skull of Death** are off-hand foci with `heal_on_kill`/`stamina_on_kill` 15. Every mob records `last_hitter` in `take_damage`; the frontier reads it in `_on_enemy_defeated`, the delve in `_on_devil_died`, the arena in `_on_killed`. Only the killer's body refills (`ArenaActor.apply_kill_refill`). Skulls pay nothing while a two-hander, bow, staff, shield or dual pair occupies the hands.
- Damage scale choke points: melee inside `Archer.melee_damage` (read by `Combat._on_melee_started` and the ability cues), ranged/throw at the spawn callsites (`Combat.damage_for(...,scale)` / `throw_damage(...,scale)`), and ability damage inside `AbilityRules.damage_for` (arrows and spells use range, blade skills use melee). `ranger_clone` and every mob keep the unscaled formulas.
- Attack speed divides the bow draw and the melee clip durations by `attack_rate`; `charge_seconds()` is the single knob for both the DRAWING branch and throws. `AbilityRunner.activate` multiplies every cooldown by `AbilityRules.cooldown_scale(rig)`, so the echo ring shortens the real timer rather than only the HUD text.
- Persistence is unchanged v4: neck/ring ids were already in `equipped`.

### Armor slots

- Armor is five independently equipped slots: **head, chest, hands, legs, feet**. `ArmorData.SLOTS` owns the order, `SET_IDS` is `leather/mail/plate/cloth/makeshift`, and the item ids are `armor_<set>_<slot>`. Each item's `kind` is its slot, so an armor equip replaces exactly one slot. A legacy whole-set id (`armor_leather`, `armor_mail`, `armor_plate`) is **migration-only**: it is expanded to five pieces when a v1 save loads and is not equippable from the backpack.
- Reduction runs on two channels. A head hit resolves the helmet alone against the 2x head zone (`ArmorData.head_reduction`), so a 50% plate sallet turns a headshot back into a normal hit and a 75% one would leave half of it; every other hit sums the four body pieces (`ArmorData.body_reduction`). Each set's body figure splits 1 : 1 : 0.3 : 0.3 over chest, legs, hands and feet (`BODY_WEIGHTS`, divisor `BODY_WEIGHT_TOTAL` 2.6), so full plate is 11.5/11.5/3.5/3.5 = 30%. Rows: leather 20/10, mail 30/25, plate 50/30, cloth 5/5, makeshift 20/10. `Archer.armor_reduction(headshot)`/`damage_reduction(headshot)` and every controller must pass the hit's zone; do not restore a single flat reduction.
- The rig's `_build_armor_meshes()` groups the imported leather meshes (`Male_Ranger_Head_Hood`, Body/Belts/Pauldron/Arms, Bracer, Legs, Boots) into slot 0 and the generated `Armor_<Set>_<Slot>` nodes into slots 1..3. `Male_Ranger_Arms` belongs to the leather chest so another chest's sleeves replace the skin arms; every non-leather chest must fully cover the arms. Keep head/eyes/brows outside every group.
- `set_armor_piece(slot,set)` preserves the other four slots; `set_armor_pieces(dict)` and `set_armor(set)` are whole-kit helpers. `armor_bits()`/`set_armor_bits()` use three bits per slot in `ArmorData.SLOTS` order, and every code above the five sets reads back as `ArmorData.NONE` (an empty slot). The network snapshot stores that packed 15-bit value in `armor`; the shield bit is `ArmorData.SHIELD_BIT` (bit 15), never the old bit 2.
- `ArenaActor.snapshot()` also carries a compact `gear` int for the visuals the bow/sword/armor fields cannot express: bit 0 off weapon active, bit 1 off kind sword (0 dagger), bit 2 off index, bit 3 staff active, bit 4 staff index, bits 5–6 focus kind (0 none / 1 tome / 2 skull), bit 7 focus index, bits 8–9 shield variant. `apply_snapshot` applies the off hand before the weapon mode so a replicated dual loadout can enter dual, then restores the staff last; the snapshot plus `gear` stays inside the clamped 1200-byte datagram (checked by `net_limits_regression`).
- The in-world paperdoll equips individual armor items from the backpack; the town Quartermaster stock is a scrollable list (20 armor pieces, 4 foci, 3 necklaces, 17 rings) whose row pool is sized from the largest keeper stock by `world_ui.stock_row_count()`, never a fixed count. Session keys still accept per-slot set changes for that match, but the arena's quick-loadout panel is hidden; the paperdoll is the visible equipment surface. Do not reintroduce three whole-set buttons.
- The ranger GLB is authored with the armor: `tools/character_armor.py` builds mail/plate/cloth and leather gloves in the rest skeleton with explicit weights, explicit UVs and embedded 512px base-color/normal textures. `Ranger_Base_Calf` is an always-visible dark hose underlayer so short sabatons never expose an empty shin when mixed with the short leather trousers; it is deliberately outside every slot group. The builder is called from `tools/prepare_character.py` after the animations are baked and before cleanup. Keep `Armor_<Set>_<Slot>` names and the `armor.generated` metadata in `ironwood_ranger.json`; the rig reads that metadata to group meshes.


### Input and rolls

- Space jumps; **Shift + Space rolls immediately** from the ground or during a jump. Repeated unmodified Space presses never roll. There is no double-tap window.
- Movement determines roll direction, then facing is the fallback. Keep the single-roll-per-airborne-cycle restriction, normal world collisions, and attack cancellation; a roll is not invulnerability.
- Both controllers pass `event.shift_pressed`. The arena's `jump` action carries an explicitly validated boolean, not a trusted client animation state.
- `6` uses a bandage and `7` drinks a healing potion in every in-match equipment panel. A bandage channels for 3 s then restores 35 health over 3 s; a potion channels for 0.5 s then restores 50 health. `Archer` plays the merged `Bandage_Wrap`/`Potion_Drink` clips and sockets a procedural roll/bottle; damage, attacks, jumps, rolls, steps, cover changes and gear changes cancel the channel. The host owns the channel and heal on authoritative actors. The delve maps `6`/`7` to the same `"consume"` action the arena uses; the frontier calls `use_consumable` directly. `tools/prepare_consumables.py` merges only the two new clips into the original GLB.
- `1` equips the selected bow; `2` equips the selected sword; `3` equips the single dagger; `4` equips or stows the shield; `5` toggles dual wield. The shield is legal with the dagger or **any one-handed sword** (`sword_index` 0 or 1, including the shortsword); switching to a bow, greatsword, staff or dual wield stows it. Bow and sword sizes are selected independently with mouse buttons, not number-key cycling.
- Dual wield requires a one-handed sword main (`sword_index` 0/1), no staff, focus or shield, and a dagger or one-handed sword off hand. Key **5** selects dagger-left; the paperdoll can select either legal off-hand weapon. LMB uses left-hand `diagonal_left`; RMB uses right-hand `diagonal_right`, never guard. Wheel up/down retain right-hand stab/overhead, and T throws the right sword. `set_sword(2)` leaves dual mode; `set_weapon("dual")` refuses a greatsword. Session weapon keys do not persist the kit.
- **LMB is the only held attack input for bows.** Release launches at most one arrow. Escape/equipment cancellation must not fire an arrow or leave a held-input latch active. Q no longer draws; E no longer interacts.
- **Q / E are grounded side steps left / right** relative to the camera (`DodgeStep`: 2.95 m travel, 0.30 s, 7 stamina, 0.55 s cooldown). Reject while airborne, rolling or cooling down; a step cancels draw/melee/throw/guard, exits cover, and ends on floor loss. Step velocity overrides normal acceleration and slide but not roll. `Archer.set_step(progress,direction)` must be fed every tick (-1 when inactive). It latches the root facing (including a 0.20 s exit hold for the decaying step velocity), opens the hips and chest into the travel, counter-turns the head toward the target, and offsets the leading/trailing feet.
- **G interacts** with a keeper, the town stash, the great firepit, a waystone, a delve cache, the rope or the open floor. The inn hatch has no prompt; it is entered by falling.
- **I opens the in-world inventory; P opens the equipment paperdoll; TAB swaps the bound hand pair** in the frontier, arena and delve. `Escape` closes the kit first; otherwise it toggles `scripts/pause_menu.gd` — the one quick menu, which frees the cursor, cancels a held attack and blocks movement, weapon keys, TAB, the wheel and mouse attacks while it is up. The menu never pauses the tree (a hosted session must keep simulating) and its exit row is scene-owned: `EXIT TO TITLE` in the world, `LEAVE MATCH` in the arena, `RETURN TO SURFACE` in the delve. It also carries **SAVE GAME** (`PlayerState.save_state`) and a windowed/fullscreen row that reports the mode the window actually took. Effect cues fire from the clock on every peer; only the authority applies `Combat.ability_strike`/`ability_blast` damage and authoritative projectiles. Weapon skills currently cost **1 stamina** and swapping the granted weapon clears that slot cooldown for testing. Bow skills solve both arms from `bow_draw` and turn the body onto the live crosshair before the cue, so every arrow leaves along that crosshair with the authored spread and an off-axis target is solved by turning, not by floating the pose. - Fighters use collision layer 4 but **mask only 1** (world). Actor-to-actor body collision is intentionally absent; `_separation_velocity` adds a bounded push only below 0.46 m centre distance so players can measure a stab without being shoved off aim. Blades and projectiles still query layer 4.

Supported full-capsule sweeps follow slopes, curved/faceted offsets and contiguous collider seams without shape/RID identity locking. Genuine edges and hard corners still stop movement. Low-cover classification also checks deeper inward geometry so an inset rock face cannot invent a top peek. Keep one endpoint latched, and normalize signed lean against its selected extent. Use the same 2 cm protected-return tolerance as arrival, not a submillimetre latch that terrain recovery can keep locked. Bow attacks OR automatic exposure with manual input; melee bypasses this gate by exiting cover.

Releasing that bow while tucked requests exposure again; blocked release cancels safely. A direct tucked bow draw owns exposure through RELEASE, then retucks unless RMB remains held. Stop, equipment changes, jump/roll, death/respawn and lost cover clear pending bow intent.

Preserve it through release/return and free look; do not recapture on mouse-up/Q-up. Both controllers feed `cover.view_right` before eligibility/step. Camera smoothing advances once per render frame; aim queries call `resolve()` without advancing it. Preserve swept-volume obstruction and overlap recovery.

The planted bow and staff casts keep the crouch layer. Armor remains equipment-button-only. Outside cover, ordinary crouch moves at 2.2 m/s and disables sprint. F exit preserves manual crouch. Standing checks headroom; never expand a capsule into an obstacle.

Run `cover.step(delta,direction,peek,side,fast)` every physics tick; use its returned velocity only if cover was active before the call. World-space `lean` is UP times physical rise for low cover, or the signed tangent times physical lateral exposure at tall edges, including retuck. `set_cover(active,normal,crouch_amount,peeking,lean)` runs before locomotion/roll pose updates in both modes and replicas. Keep cover data in the single physical cover snapshot, not duplicated in `get_animation_state()`; apply it before `apply_animation_state()`. Camera height follows crouch outside cover too.

Otherwise it exits cover, clears manual crouch and peek latches, restores the standing collider and performs the existing jump/roll. Apply queued jump velocity even if stance preparation changed the floor cache. Death, respawn and camp return use `cover.reset()` and clear held intent.

Reliable peek-context messages precede draw/release without fabricating RMB presses. ### Bow charge and recovery

- `Archer.BOW_DATA.draw_seconds` is the complete time to full charge: small/medium/large currently take 1/2/3 seconds. Raising is included, not an extra delay before charge begins. Base launch speeds are **66/84/102 m/s** for the shortbow/recurve/longbow; partial charge scales speed from 45% to 100% alongside damage.
- `draw_amount` drives the bar, string tension, and damage fraction. Half the draw duration means half damage; for the longbow, 1.5 seconds gives 50% of its base damage.
- Preserve the action sequence: `READY` → `DRAWING` → `HOLDING` if fully charged → `RELEASE` → `RECOVER` → `READY`. Release is also valid during `DRAWING`; holding at full charge never auto-fires.
- In cover, an early bow release sets replicated `rig.charge_frozen`: charge stops at input release while the raise clock continues. `Combat` fires once only after physical exposure and the 0.30-second raise; waiting never increases damage. Non-cover release timing remains immediate.
- There is no separate bow cooldown or movement recovery penalty. Readiness follows the actual release/re-nock animation through `get_bow_recovery_progress()`. The current follow-through is 0.28 seconds and arrow retrieval/re-nocking is 0.95 seconds.
- Every shot, including zero/partial charge, spends the nocked arrow and completes recovery before another draw. Equipment swaps, cancellation, or rolling cannot skip/restart that recovery. Canceling an unreleased draw does not spend its arrow.
- Carry leftover frame time from release into recovery. Do not add a frame-dependent extra delay at phase boundaries.
- Aim remains a world-space point from the camera ray, not target tracking or gravity compensation. Start the query at the projected actor position plus `cover.camera_height()` and exclude the shooter and the attached cover body through `CoverMovement.attached_surface()` (host-cached, replica-derived from `normal`) so top peeks can fire over the wall you are posted against. Never choose the query start from the animated muzzle: it feeds bow facing/raise back into targeting, skips close crosshair targets and makes shoulder convergence unstable. Aim the bow from its cheek anchor. Before a cover release, sweep the actual nock-to-tip shaft with finite radius and an initial-overlap check. A fresh exposure gets `Combat.SHAFT_SETTLE_GRACE` (0.15 s) for its lean/bow pose to settle before a blocked shaft is treated as permanent; a settled obstruction still cancels pending release/frozen charge.
- A released arrow travels on the center crosshair ray, not a converging line from the bow tip. `Combat.set_camera_ray` validates a muzzle point on that ray (preferring the point nearest the rendered tip) with a spawn-overlap check, a clear path to the aim point, and a clear link back to the rendered tip; if any check fails, it clears the override and the arrow uses the rendered tip. The arena sends the client camera-ray origin with aim actions and movement, validates it within 12 m of the actor, and replicates the captured visual start in the shot event. `Arrow` blends its rendered shaft from that visual start to the authoritative path over `VISUAL_BLEND_SECONDS` (0.12 s) and snaps it to the impact point. Ordinary projectile collision alone applies damage. Never remove the fallback: a camera-ray spawn buried in posted cover must not shoot through it.

### Damage zones

- `scripts/hitbox.gd` owns the **2x head multiplier** and the zone profiles for humanoids, the wolf, the cinder devil and every imported mob family. A head hit resolves against the helmet's head channel, never the body kit. `head_zone_radius(authored, body_radius)` keeps a zone from ever being narrower than the capsule an arrow stops on (`maxf(authored, body_radius + HEAD_SURFACE_MARGIN)`); mob `head_hit_profile()` implementations pass their own capsule radius, so a shaft that lands on the head height of the visible body is a headshot. Head zones are logical damage volumes against the single physical body capsule; do not add extra collision shapes, layers, areas or RIDs. That keeps arrows, blades, movement and cover on the existing authoritative collision path.
- Zones are upright cylinders around animated joints: arena actors read the rig's `Head` bone, the wolf reads its imported `head` bone and the cinder devil reads its imported `head` bone on an authored 2.11 m skull. Crouch, cover lean, rolls and the wolf's bite move the zone with the skeleton.
- Diagonal cleaves always use base body damage, never incidental headshots. Arrows classify their actual ray point with `Hitbox.is_headshot`; precision stabs/overheads use `Hitbox.blade_headshot` with swept blade segments and radius, never a shape query's arbitrary rest point. Thrown weapons retain their projectile head-zone path. Apply the 2x multiplier before the head channel's reduction only for these precision/projectile contacts.
- Receivers take a `headshot` boolean: `ArenaActor.take_damage` / `receive_melee`, `DelveActor.take_damage`, `RangerClone.take_damage` and `enemy.gd.take_damage`. Apply the multiplier, then the hit's own armor channel, before the frontier damage label. The frontier ranger's `main.gd.take_damage` has no zone, so it always uses the body channel. Arena damage stays host-authoritative; do not add a headshot field to snapshots or trust client classification.
- `melee_roll_regression` intentionally expects the greatsword's vertical cut to deal 2x and the one-handed cuts to deal base damage. Do not flatten those expectations to a single value.

### Blood and wounds

- Blood is **presentation only** and must never gate damage, hits, blocks, stamina or network authority. Spawn it only where damage was actually applied: unmitigated melee contact, a projectile that damaged a body, a real `take_damage`, and a replicated health/weapon-blood delta. Parry, standard block and shield block spawn no spray; a weak dagger block keeps only the half-damage wound.
- `BloodFx` is the static entry point (`impact`, `wound`, `stain_weapon`, `stain_weapon_node`, `stain_projectile`). `field_for` lazily creates one `BloodField` under `get_tree().current_scene` (tree root in script-mode tests). Do not add a global autoload or persist the field across scenes.
- **60 seconds** is the contract: `BloodFx.LIFETIME = 60.0`, fading over the final `FADE_SECONDS = 3.0` and then freeing. Surface patches fade through `GeometryInstance3D.transparency`; rig/weapon coatings shrink. Budgets are `BloodField.max_surface_stains = 160` world patches, 10 wounds per rig and 8 coatings per weapon; recycle oldest-first. Do not remove the budgets without measuring a crowd fight.
- The renderer is **GL Compatibility**, which has no `Decal` support. Keep ground blood as physics-conformed `ArrayMesh` patches and body/weapon blood as bone- or weapon-parented meshes. Never replace this with `Decal` or a `next_pass` stain shader.
- Body blood attaches to the nearest `Skeleton3D` bone through a reused, per-bone `BoneAttachment3D` (`BloodBone_<index>`), which `BloodField` frees once its last stain expires; never leave empty attachments behind. Weapon blood attaches to the drawn weapon node so it follows swings, sockets and equipment swaps. Player weapon anchors come from `Archer.blood_weapon_anchor(hand)`; enemies expose `blood_rig()`, `blood_scale()` and `blood_weapon_anchor(hand)`, `scripts/cinder_devil_visual.gd` provides `blood_anchor()`/`weapon_anchor()`, and the delve's primitive roles keep the same pair in `scripts/devil_visual.gd`.
- Body stains must lie **on the garment**. `BloodSurface.body_for(rig)` measures the visible skinned cloth once per rig (bone radial profile over 24 x 12 cells) and caches it per `body_for` signature; swap-invalidating is the visible **skinned mesh name set**, so armour changes remeasure while a raised bow, a dropped weapon or a death pose do not. `add_patch` builds a small grid whose **every vertex is projected back onto the shell** (`surface_point`, clamped to the measured strip, 3 mm `LIFT`), so the sheet hugs a curved breastplate instead of floating in the tangent plane; a vertex with no cloth collapses onto the patch centre instead of hanging beside the body. The profile takes the max radius per cell, so the stain lands on the outermost layer already — there is no separate pose-skinned garment sample or per-patch `outward` lift. Primitive rigs (the delve's depth roles) are measured as their own triangle soup and parent stains to the limb `MeshInstance3D`. Never go back to free-floating flattened spheres: they read as blobs beside the body.
- Splash size is clamped by `BloodSurface.patch_extent` to the measured strip; a patch bigger than its strip collapses into a flat sheet. `BloodField._stain_body` lays one main soak plus a few smaller satellites that **overlap** it (3 x 3 grids each), so a hit reads as one irregular flat stain; keep the satellites overlapping — spread them onto the rim and the crisp rims read as a star of separate discs.
- The soak texture is a **flat unshaded dye**: a solid crimson core with a thin crisp rim and a few faint specks, not a feathered alpha ramp. It is `SHADING_MODE_UNSHADED` so a dark stain does not vanish in shadow; do not go back to lit feathered splats, which read as a floating bib.
- The frontier ranger leaves **bloody footprints**: `scripts/bloody_feet.gd` is the one tracker, called from `main.gd._physics_process` (`_bloody_feet.update(delta,player,archer)`) and from `arena_actor.gd`'s `step()` (`update(delta,self,rig)`), so the frontier, the arena and the delve all print. It asks the scene's `BloodField` whether either sole is in a pool (`is_in_blood`, floor-plane radius plus a 0.8 m height band, so a body on a roof or a balcony above the pool is not in it) and, on the next footfall, calls `footprint(at, travel, strength)`. A footfall is the foot's descending pass through its tracked peak; debounce it to one print per `0.30` s per foot, or the run clip's bobbing lays a smear. Each step spends `0.15` of the `_feet_blood` the pool gave (1.0), so a soaking is ~6 prints and stops. Prints are unshaded 25 x 11 cm conformed strips with the heel/arch/ball foot texture, share the world-spatter budget and fade with it at 60 s. Keep the tracker presentation-only and mode-neutral: do not give a controller its own copy, and remember an arena/delve client runs no authoritative `step()`, so its prints come only from the host-side body it actually steps.
- Projectiles are special-cased: `BloodField.stain_projectile` builds a short curved smear on the shaft behind the head (plus two specks), sized from the shaft, not the blade path. Arrows must never receive a blade-sized coating — that is the "goo" look.
- The first blood event on a rig costs a one-off measurement (~30 ms on the ranger; cached afterwards) and each later wound is ~1-2 ms. Keep it there: remeasuring per wound or per frame is a stutter.
- Each wound and each weapon coating is merged through `SurfaceTool` into **one** `MeshInstance3D`; do not add a separate node per droplet. Ground splats stay one mesh per stain. This keeps a bloody crowd inside the draw budget.
- Networked clients derive wounds from snapshot health deltas and weapon coatings from the snapshot `weapon_blood` tally (`ArenaActor.note_weapon_blood`, inherited by `DelveActor`). Do not add a dedicated blood RPC or trust client damage claims for blood.

### Thrown weapons

- Hold **T** with a sword or dagger to charge; release to throw one copy. Melee stays on LMB, wheel up (stab) and wheel down (overhead). `Archer.begin_throw()` uses dedicated phases `T_DRAW` → `T_HOLD` → `T_RELEASE` → `T_RECOVER` so the charge never collides with bow phases or melee and the snapshot phase stays short. Keep the phase names in `Combat.advance`, `get_attack_interval`, `update_aim`, both HUDs, and the animation-state replica path.
- Charge time is the equipped weapon's `seconds` (1/2/3 s swords, 0.5 s dagger). Damage is `base damage × clamp(charge,0,1)` via `Combat.throw_damage`; the projectile uses `ThrownWeapon`, the arrow's gravity (-4), swept ray collision, head-zone multiplier, and stops on the first contact. It is not `receive_melee`, so guards never parry a throw.
- Spent throw timing is `Archer.RELEASE_SECONDS` + `Archer.RECOVER_SECONDS` (0.28 + 0.95). `Archer.get_recovery_progress()` covers both bow and throw phases. Throwing gates on `Combat.melee_progress >= 1`, like melee, because it uses the same weapon.
- Endless supply is deliberate: throwing never consumes a paperdoll or backpack item, and the equipped weapon never disappears permanently. `_sync_weapon_visibility()` hides it only during `T_RELEASE` and restores it in recovery. Do not decrement the backpack or mutate `sword_index`/dagger availability.
- Throw pose samples the existing authored melee clip (`_throw_clip_time`): the chamber through charge, the authored strike on release, and the follow-through as recovery. Do not invent a procedural throw or reuse bow arm IK. The greatsword carry/guard overlay is disabled during throw phases.
- Cover behavior mirrors the bow: `Combat.begin_throw`/`release_throw` use the shared exposure, frozen-charge, and `cover_aim_ready` gates. `_cover_throw_clear` sweeps the exposed hand down the aim line for 1.2 m; obstruction cancels instead of throwing through cover.
- Arena: clients send `"throw"` / `"throw_release"` (aim + observed snapshot tick) and the host validates them like draw/release. `ArenaActor.thrown_weapon` feeds `Arena._on_thrown_weapon`, which spawns the authoritative projectile and broadcasts `_throw_event` reliably. Replicas spawn visual-only projectiles with `authoritative_damage = false`.

### Swords, daggers and state isolation

- Standard diagonal attacks sweep a frontal sector chronologically through the active stroke, not a thin blade-only contact and not an instantaneous whole-arc hit. Per-hand rig APIs are `melee_variant(hand)`, `is_diagonal_swing(hand)`, `melee_cut_progress(hand)` (0..1 through the clip), `melee_cut_curve(hand)` (release curve), `melee_half_angle(hand)` (radians), and `melee_reach(hand)`. Dagger: 0.42 s, 1.2 m, 75-degree half-angle. Shortsword/arming/greatsword: 0.60/0.78/1.02 s, 1.8/2.1/2.5 m, 80/85/90-degree half-angles. Staffs: 1.6/1.5 m middle-grip reach, same greatsword damage/timing and 90-degree half-angle. Bigger weapons visibly cock the hands up and behind the striking shoulder, pitch the blade up outside the active sector, hold the loaded pose for a beat, then cut down across the torso and follow through low, with larger arcs and the greatsword support hand staying on the hilt.
- Diagonals run one shared clock: load to 30%, release 30-45%, follow-through 45-65%, cushion to 75%, recovery to 100%. `SWING_LOAD_END`/`SWING_FOLLOW_END`/`SWING_SETTLE_END` and `_swing_drive()` define that clock; `melee_cut_curve()` is the same curve the visible arm uses, so never give `Combat._sweep_cleave` a different angular curve. The kinetic chain is explicit in `SWING_CHAIN_PELVIS`/`CHEST`/`CLAVICLE`/`BLADE`: each joint runs the same curve on its own offset, so the pelvis turns first, the chest follows, the clavicle drags the shoulder and the hand and blade tip arrive last. `DIAGONAL_BLADE` owns the hilt path (`yaw_load`/`yaw_cross`/`rad_load`/`rad_cross`/`fwd_load`/`fwd_cross`/`up_load`/`up_cross`), the blade aim (`blade_load`/`blade_cross`/`tip_load`/`tip_down`) and the body shares (`pelvis_*`, `chest_*`, `lean_*`, `clavicle`). `yaw_cross`/`blade_cross`/`tip_cross` are magnitudes mirrored by the variant side; the hand yaw must actually sign-flip through the drive, so the hilt crosses the centre line while the tip passes the sector edge. A free off hand reaches forward as a counterbalance; a shield or focus keeps its guard.
- Diagonal body motion samples the existing `Sword_Overhead` for the plant, hip drop and knee drive, but the torso belongs to the swing: `_apply_melee_swing_path` rebuilds `spine_01`/`spine_02`/`spine_03` as absolute rest-frame yaw and pitch shares (0.34/0.68/1.0 of the chest value) instead of stacking deltas on the authored chop, so the chest extends over the trailing hip on the load and crunches through the strike instead of inheriting the overhead's forward hunch. The head gives back most of that yaw and pitch (`head_yaw`/`head_pitch`) so the gaze stays on the target. Mirror the leg pairs for `diagonal_left`. On a stationary actor the stance is pinned, not followed: `_swing_stance` captures both ankles and the neutral hip offset on the first frame, the pelvis turns and re-solves the legs onto those ankles, and its horizontal travel is clamped to 0.08 m of the anchor — the authored overhead's 0.3 m lunge and 0.4 m hip slide must not step the actor out of his circle. The clamp and both ankles are handed back to the authored carry across the recovery (`handoff`), otherwise the pose snaps home in one frame and the elbows jump. The chest leans forward (positive `lean_*`), never back; a negative pitch means extension. While moving, crouched or sliding the pelvis is locked and the entire coil goes into the spine plus the striking clavicle (`clavicle_r` for the right hand, `clavicle_l` for the left) — never rotate the pelvis under a locomotion clip. Include primary hand and variant in pose-transition identity so overlapping cuts blend instead of popping.
- The arm solve owns an explicit backswing: `yaw_load` parks the hilt behind the striking shoulder (`fwd_load` near zero), `up_load` lifts the wrist above that shoulder and `blade_load` holds the blade up and outside the active sector through the whole load, so the travel to the chamber comes from the path and never from a weapon-frame blend that would sweep the tip forward first. The blade direction is slerped out of the pose's own carry direction: one-handed cuts load through an outward/up waypoint, two-handed and backhand (`cross_body`) chambers lift over the shoulder with a rearward bias. A shared two-handed hilt also keeps 0.30 m of clearance ahead of the shoulder line so pulling it back cannot bury the hands in the chest. The hilt arc still uses its own outward/up control points, never the low forward recovery control that would erase the pullback. `action_pose_regression` asserts the cocked wrist height, rearward hand, blade behind the body plane, the tip never pushing forward past the shoulder during the load, the hilt crossing the centre line and the head keeping its gaze for every variant and weapon size.
- Two-handed diagonal wrist paths are centred between the shoulders, not on the sword-arm shoulder. Solve the shared hilt within both arms' reach while keeping it ahead of the torso; the support hand approaches around the ribs and acquires the hilt through the first 35% of the load, not at the top. During the cocked load the sword hilt keeps at least 0.34 m of front clearance and the support hand grips close (0.05 m) before widening to 0.20 m through the cut; the staff uses its own 0.28 m floor. Drive the support elbow to its exterior bend from the start of acquisition. Uncoil the torso before handing hilt depth and elbow direction back to the authored carry. Validate actual forearm thickness against the skinned torso, not just reachable wrists or correct palm-to-hilt distance.
- Staffs are held by the middle, not like a two-handed sword, and they do not reuse the two-handed sword's swing shape. `_staff_mesh_offset()` shifts the shaft so the right hand sits 0.16 m below its midpoint; `STAFF_GRIP_DEPTH` puts the support hand 0.32 m above it on the same shaft, both hands around the centre. The visual socket uses that offset, but the support solve works in hand space (`sword_hand * _sword_grip`) so the grip depth stays on the shaft. `DIAGONAL_STAFF` is its own spec: the hands orbit little and piston forward (`fwd_cross` 0.54 m) while the shaft barrells through a much larger angle (`blade_load` to `blade_cross`), so the leading end finishes ahead and low and the rear end counterweights up and behind the hands. `STAFF_REACH` (1.6/1.5 m) supplies damage and the catalog/HUD stats. The authored overhead and thrust slide the shaft to an end grip over the first 38% of their duration and return to the middle on recovery; a centred shaft would put its lower half through the floor at the low overhead finish.
- Cleaves catch short enemies and multiple bodies across the front, excluding the rear/out-of-reach targets. Each target gets its own world-occlusion check: a blocked target must not stop other visible victims from being hit. Damage is base body damage (14 dagger, 30/38/50 swords), once per victim per hand per swing, active-stroke only and host-only. Preserve guards, attack parries, clashes, cancellation, independent recovery and coarse-frame coverage. Explicit overheads keep authored 1/2/3 s sword timing; precision stabs keep their 0.5 s aim-directed blade sweeps and headshots. Throw charge durations are unchanged.
- Melee visibility owns `_melee_sight_query`, with collision mask 1. Camera projectile-lane checks use a separate query and include `target_mask`; never share their mutable collision mask with melee. A target must not occlude its own cleave, and other fighters are not world cover. The broad phase is a body-height cylinder from -0.25 to +1.85 m relative to the attacker; angular samples have 12 degrees of forgiveness, clamped to the front half-plane.
- The dagger remains a separate one-size weapon. LMB alternates diagonal cuts; precision wheel attacks use the authored `Dagger_Stab` clip (0.5 s, 10/60–17/60 strike window), with 14 damage, 1.2 m reach and 0.34 m blade. The precision arm/socket solve follows the crosshair without a pelvis lunge or auto-target; high aim stays high and low aim angles down. Only precision attacks use blade-contact headshots; do not apply stab IK to a diagonal or give the dagger a two-handed grip.
- A dagger guard is a **weak block**: it never parries, takes a hit at `WEAK_BLOCK_DAMAGE_SCALE` (50%), spends `DAGGER_BLOCK_STAMINA` (30), and does not neutralize or stagger the attacker. Swords keep the 0.25 s parry / full held block. Dagger-guard presentation reuses `Sword_Block_Loop_OneHand`. A dagger never participates in sword clashes.
- **Weapon passives** are a second attack rate beside trinket haste. `scripts/item_passives.gd` is one table keyed by weapon class; `Archer.variant_attack_rate(hand,variant)` returns the multiplier for the class the hand is actually swinging, `_duration_for` multiplies it with `attack_rate()`, and `_melee_window` scales the authored stab window by the shortened swing so the drive never outlives the recovery. The dagger's **Prison shank** row is +200% on `stab` only: the wheel-up lunge resolves to **0.167 s** (20 `MELEE_SAMPLE_SECONDS` substeps, one sweep or more, one damage instance), while the wheel-down chop and both diagonal cuts keep their authored timing and a sword's stab is untouched. Key a row by weapon class, never by catalog id: the snapshot carries `weapon`/`off_weapon_kind` and no item ids, so an id-keyed row would leave every replica swinging at the wrong speed. A new variant key belongs in `ItemPassives.VARIANT_KEYS` and its rendering together.
- **Dual wield** owns independent `_left_melee_time` / `_right_melee_time` and recovery clocks. Either hand can start while the other winds up, strikes or recovers, but cannot restart itself until complete. Normal left/right inputs choose `diagonal_left` / `diagonal_right` and use the actual hand's dagger or sword profile. Right-hand action drives the primary body when active; left-only phase stays `MELEE_L`, and right phases are `MELEE`, `MELEE_DL`, `MELEE_DR`, `MELEE_ST`. Layer the left cut without overwriting the active right arm; preserve torso clearance, hand sockets and mirrored `_sword_grip_left`.
- `melee_started(hand)` and `melee_sweep(hand,...)` carry the hand. `Combat` keeps a separate swing record and progress for each hand (`left_melee_progress`/`right_melee_progress`) and emits both hands' sweeps independently. The public `melee_progress` remains the single-weapon/right recovery and a display alias for dual. A standard block calls `abort_swing(hand)` so only the contacting hand resets; the other keeps its clock. A parry calls `stagger`, which cancels both hands. `abort_melee_to_neutral` clears both for clashes/cancellation.
- Replication keeps existing snapshot keys and ENet's MTU budget. Right variants ride in `phase` (`MELEE`, `MELEE_DL`, `MELEE_DR`, `MELEE_ST`); left-only remains `MELEE_L`. During melee the existing `draw` scalar packs an integer: low two bits encode the left variant (`overhead=0`, `diagonal_left=1`, `diagonal_right=2`, `stab=3`), upper bits hold left elapsed milliseconds plus one (zero means idle). `time` carries the primary clock, including full-precision left time in `MELEE_L`. Replicas derive both durations from each hand's weapon and variant; do not add snapshot keys or assume the left hand is always a dagger stab.
- Dual wield **cannot guard**: `Combat.start_block`/`Archer.set_blocking` reject it and `can_equip_shield` is false. Instead, `Combat.resolve_melee_defense` returns `DEFENSE_PARRY` when `rig.weapon == "dual"`, `rig.in_strike_window()` is true for either hand, and the attacker is in front. The parry applies no damage or stamina cost, does not cancel the dual wielder's swing, and staggers the attacker through the existing `PARRY_STAGGER` path. Only dual wield has this attack parry; front facing and the authored strike window are both required.
- The shield (`scripts/shield.gd`) is an `AnimatableBody3D` on collision layer 8, built procedurally and hidden while stowed. Its local -Z is the face; `Archer._update_shield_pose()` carries it on the left hand when idle, raises it at the chest/head when `_blocking`, and during a shield skill presents the disc ahead of the body: its plane is pushed clear of the chest, head and legs (measured bone extents in `ABILITY_SHIELD_CHEST_CLEAR`/`_HEAD_CLEAR`/`_LEG_CLEAR`, leg bones height-gated) and the left arm is solved onto the pushed wrist, so a clip that draws the hand back cannot carry the boss through the torso or helmet. Every arrow source includes layer 8 — player bows (`main.gd`, `arena.gd`, `delve.gd`), devil bows (`enemy.gd`) and ranger clones (`ranger_clone.gd`) — so a shield block stops enemy fire too: a shield hit applies no damage (the collider has no `take_damage`), and the arrow reparents to the shield; an arrow into a player body damages and reparents to the player body (`ironwood_player` meta). The arrow keeps its shaft visible and times out with the existing age limit. The shield is not in the movement, aim, cover or melee masks.
- Arena shield state is packed into `ArmorData.SHIELD_BIT` (bit 10) of the snapshot `armor` field, above the two-bit-per-slot armor state, decoded by `apply_snapshot`, so the maximum-name snapshot stays inside the clamped datagram. Clients send `"shield"` as a boolean; the host stops the guard and validates eligibility. Replicas raise the shield through the replicated `GUARD` phase and the decoded state; the shield collider exists on every peer.
- `Combat` snapshots the swing weapon, damage and duration at `melee_started`, so switching loadouts cannot change an in-flight hit or its recovery clock. Keep `get_blade_base/get_blade_tip`, `melee_radius`, and the shared sweep path weapon-aware.
- `Combat.melee_progress` is independent of bow readiness. Switching to a bow must neither block bow charging with sword recovery nor erase that recovery when switching back.
- `attack_progress` is presentation/replication state; do not treat it as a universal cooldown gate. `ArenaActor.revive()` resets both progress fields and rig/roll state.
- New attacks require solid ground and cannot start while rolling. Preserve cancellation before melee impact and the greatsword's two-hand grip.
- A valid `Combat.begin_melee()` immediately exits sticky cover and clears the rig's cover pose before the first swing pose. No peek prerequisite, deferred melee, automatic retuck, teleport or forced standing. Preserve manual crouch and collider height; rejected attacks leave attachment unchanged. Movement remains free and F is required to reattach. Per-target diagonal occlusion and precision blade/world collision still block damage through cover.
- `melee_started()` resets per-swing victim state. `melee_sweep(previous_base, previous_tip, current_base, current_tip, radius)` supplies chronological world-space blade samples from the same pose used for the visible weapon. Preserve source exclusion, authoritative-only damage, cancellation, and coarse-frame contact coverage.
- Arena melee resolves through `ArenaActor.receive_melee`, not unconditional `take_damage`, preserving front-guard timing. Arrow damage stays on `take_damage`. A valid melee contact while both sword users are active and mutually facing is a **clash**: zero damage, both swings reset to READY with recovery cleared, both bounce apart, and sparks/clang replicate. This applies to cleave-sector and precision contacts; do not add sword-to-sword geometry or let ordering turn it into damage trades. `clash_cooldown` only stops bot lockstep, not human re-attacks.
- Held guards use dedicated character-only loops from `assets/characters/ironwood_ranger.glb`: `Sword_Block_Loop_OneHand` for short/arming swords and `Sword_Block_Loop` for the greatsword. Both are 120-sample / 1.983-second breathing loops authored by `tools/prepare_character.py` with planted feet; the two-hand clip solves the support palm onto the actual hilt. Never fall back to `Sword_Idle`/`Sword_Idle_OneHand` for blocking. The clips animate only the Ranger skeleton; the sword stays the runtime socketed weapon. When crouched or sliding, pass `_blocking` into the lower-body-only crouch/slide layer so those legs cannot overwrite the guard arms. `Sword_Block_Loop*` are validated at 240 Hz for forearm clearance, support-hilt error and runtime-blade path; the JSON's `block_authoring` records wrist/elbow/blade targets.
- Block state is host-authoritative in the arena. Clients send `{pressed, forward}` on RMB press/release; the host validates and applies `set_block`. Guard presentation rides in the animation snapshot's `phase` as `GUARD`, which replicas map back to gameplay `READY`; do not add a `blocking` or `block_time` snapshot field, because the maximum-name snapshot already sits at the clamped boundary. Guard timing stays host-only: replica HUDs show **GUARDING** without inventing a parry window. Input age >0.65, cursor release and focus loss stop the guard. The frontier routes `enemy.gd`'s `attacked(attacker, amount, victim)` through `_on_enemy_attack`, so parries call `stagger(0.8)` on the wolf or cinder devil instead of applying damage; a remote seat victim resolves through `ArenaActor.receive_melee`.

### Datagram budget

- Godot's `ENetMultiplayerPeer` has **no MTU setting** (`create_server` takes only port, client count, channels and bandwidth); `SceneMultiplayer.max_sync_packet_size`/`max_delta_packet_size` govern `MultiplayerSynchronizer`, which this project does not use. The clamp lives in `scripts/net_limits.gd`: **`MTU` 1200 bytes**, **`PAYLOAD` 1100** after a 100-byte RPC/ENet envelope, and `batches(entries,fixed)` greedily packing entries until the encoded argument list would pass the budget. Nothing may encode past `MTU`.
- A Godot array encodes as an 8-byte header plus each element's own bytes, while a dictionary re-encodes every key name, so the packer sums values and measures the fixed arguments once. Wire blocks carry **values positionally**: `Archer.pack_animation_state`, `CoverMovement.pack_snapshot` and `AbilityRunner.pack_snapshot` return arrays in their `KEYS` order, and every `apply_*`/`unpack_*` accepts the array or the local dictionary. Do not put keyed dictionaries back on the fighter wire.
- A body driven by a remote peer adds exactly one acknowledgement block to its snapshot: `ack = [frame, pose, velocity]`, positionally, like every other wire value — the movement frame the authority consumed last, the pose that frame produced and the velocity it ended with (so a snap converges in one step). Measured **~1.09 KB** with a maximum-length name; the fighter snapshot already sits against the budget, so it stays one block, not three keys.
- Measured live payloads: fighter snapshot **~1.07 KB**, heaviest mob/clone snapshot **~0.6 KB**, a 30-body delve wave split into **~4 datagrams**. `net_limits_regression` fails if any payload crosses `PAYLOAD`; `delve_network_regression` and `world_network_regression` exercise the split end to end.
- A delve wave now arrives as several same-tick `_devil_snapshots`/`_clone_snapshots` datagrams, so those receivers reject only `tick < last`; applying a batch twice is idempotent. Reverting to `tick <= last` silently drops every batch after the first.

### Prediction, reconciliation and lag compensation

- **Client-side prediction.** The peer that owns a fighter sets `predicting = true` and calls `predicted_step(delta, actors)` once per physics frame; `step()` then integrates locally and pushes the resolved motion frame into `_prediction`. Prediction is suspended while `cover.active`: the attached slide, the crouch transition and the exposure timing are authoritative, and a locally advanced cover state fights every snapshot. The rig's animation stays snapshot-driven in every case — predict translation, never a pose.
- **Server reconciliation.** `accept_network_input()` **queues** the client frame (up to `INPUT_QUEUE_LIMIT`, 0.2 s of frames); the authority's `step()` consumes exactly one per tick (`_consume_input_frame`), integrates any backlog behind it (`_drain_input_backlog`), and `_commit_acknowledgement()` runs at the **end** of the tick, recording the pose the newest consumed frame produced. A tick that brought no frame **holds** the pose (`_hold_position`) instead of repeating the last intent: a repeated intent walks the authority ahead of the frames that own the body, and every invented frame came back as a correction. Only a body a peer's numbered frames actually drive is held (`frame_driven`); the host's own fighter, a bot or a test fixture simulates every tick. Movement packets also repeat the previous `INPUT_REDUNDANCY` frames (`input_history`), so one dropped datagram cannot leave the authority permanently short of that peer's travel.
- **The correction is a displacement, not a pose.** `reconcile()` ignores an acknowledgement at or below `_prediction.acked` (the authority repeats its last ack whenever it has no fresh input), and skips anything within `reconcile_tolerance()` — two frames of the body's own travel. Beyond that it applies `shift = pose - record_at(ack + 1).pos` to the present body and **drops the unacknowledged ring**; it never re-simulates the window. Replaying frames from the authoritative pose cannot reproduce a path the local simulation produced through collisions and slopes the replay did not hit, and every mismatch came back as a pop of the whole window. It also never calls `move_and_slide()`: outside `_physics_process` that integrates against the idle frame's delta. The correction is applied inside the physics frame (`_pending_state`, consumed by `predicted_step`), not in the RPC callback.
- **The model glides over a correction.** `_rig_offset` accumulates the displacement the body took, and `_glide_rig()` draws the rig at `_rig_drawn` — the previous frame's drawn pose moved toward `body + _rig_offset` by at most `RIG_DRAW_STEP` (0.35 m) a frame, with the offset bleeding at `RIG_GLIDE_SPEED`. The simulated body takes the authority's verdict at once; the character the player sees steps into place. `revive()` snaps both.
- **The boom eases.** `main.gd::_update_camera` keeps `_camera_boom` and zooms it toward the collision-resolved length at `CAMERA_BOOM_IN`/`CAMERA_BOOM_OUT` (18/5 m/s) instead of adopting it. Snapping the boom in whenever a trunk crossed the line and back out when it cleared read as the whole view shaking.
- **Entity interpolation.** Remote fighters, and every mob, devil and clone, keep a `SnapshotTimeline` and render `delay_ticks` behind the newest state. `advance()` nudges the playout clock toward `newest - delay` at a bounded rate and only resynchronises past `CATCHUP_TICKS`; pushes feed a jitter estimate that widens the delay, and a jump no recorded velocity explains restarts the buffer (revive, blink, scene hand-off). `mob_timeline()` sets the mob floor at two snapshot intervals and derives a velocity from sample spacing when the wire carries none.
- **A replica must re-capture its swing stance.** `Archer._update_pose` pins the pelvis and both ankles to `_swing_stance`, which is captured **in world space** the first time a stationary melee frame is posed. `begin_melee()` clears `_swing_stance_valid`, but a replica never calls it — its clips come from `apply_animation_state()` — so a replica that is not cleared there keeps its hips at the spot of its **first** swing for the rest of the session, and every later swing snaps the model back to it (the reported "snaps back to where I killed the first enemy, at the start of each swing"). `apply_animation_state()` clears the flag at every replicated swing start (variant change, elapsed time reset, a new right-hand variant) and when the melee ends.
- **Tick rate.** `project.godot` pins `physics/common/physics_ticks_per_second=60`, and every tick number in the protocol (frame sequence, acknowledgement tick, view tick, interpolation delays) counts those physics ticks. Netcode runs from `_physics_process`; `_process` only advances a render-rate interpolation buffer.
- **Simulated link.** `NetReplication.LagQueue` holds the high-frequency streams (`_world_move`, `_world_action`, `_world_seat`, `_world_mobs`) for `--net-lag` milliseconds ± `--net-jitter` before they are applied, so a localhost session behaves like a Tailscale one. Localhost hides prediction, interpolation and rewind defects: at 0.1 ms the past and the present are the same frame. It is off unless the flags are passed. `--net-diag` prints one `NET_DIAG` line a second (role, slot, predicting, corrections, drift, the worst model and boom step of that second, unacknowledged frames, held packets, health), so a report from a real match carries numbers.
- **Backward reconciliation.** Do **not** rewind by moving bodies: Godot rebuilds a body's broadphase entry during the physics step, so a body teleported inside a frame is invisible to `intersect_shape`/`intersect_ray` until the next one. `LagCompensation` stores each damageable's own capsule per tick and answers geometric tests instead. `Combat` resolves the present hit and then adds the victims it had in the shooter's frame (`_lag_melee_strike`, `_lag_area_strike`) and refuses a precision contact the shooter's frame cannot explain (`refuses`); `arrow.gd`/`thrown_weapon.gd` carry the shooter's view lag and resolve historical contacts at impact. `lag_ticks = 0` on the host's own actions, so offline and local combat is unchanged.

### Open world session (shared town and frontier)

- `main.gd` carries `network_role` (`offline`/`host`/`client`), `local_slot`, `_peer_slots`, `_seats`, `_snapshot_ticks` and the ENet client/server lifecycle. Modes are exactly `world-host` and `world-join`; CLI `--world-host --world-port --world-name --world-join <address>` wins over `Session`. Host prints `WORLD_HOST_READY port=`, `WORLD_PEER_JOINED slot=`, `WORLD_PEER_LEFT slot=`; clients print `WORLD_CLIENT_READY slot=`. Three clients join the host for four rangers; a fourth connection is refused. There is no host migration. A host's hatch descent is the one scene change that keeps the session: it broadcasts `Session.descend_party`, the clients load `delve.tscn`, and both ends adopt the same `ENetMultiplayerPeer` (kept on the root MultiplayerAPI) instead of reconnecting. Host disconnect still shows the client message and continues solo with live mobs.
- Remote rangers are `ArenaActor` seats. The host creates seats 1..3, sets `bounds = WORLD_LIMIT-2`, `combat.target_mask = 2`, `damage_enabled = authoritative`, and steps them from validated intents (`_world_move`, `_world_action`, `_set_seat_aim`, `_apply_seat_action`) exactly like the arena; the host's own ranger stays `main.gd`'s controller. Its snapshot uses the same 26-key fighter payload with `Seat.gear_bits_for(archer)`. `_apply_seat_action` also handles `respawn` (seat revive at the town gate) and `rest` (camp-heal, validated within 3.8 m of the campfire).
- A client predicts **its own seat's movement** and nothing else: `_client_step` integrates the frame it is about to send through the same `step()` the host runs, then sends that frame with its sequence number and its view tick **every physics frame** (not every 0.05 s — a 20 Hz input hold makes the authority's turn lag the player's by a quarter second). It never simulates mobs, damage or projectiles; `send_action()` still sends every attack/throw/block/peek/step/jump/cover/crouch/ability/consume/equip/loadout intent, and only `jump` and `step` are mirrored locally; `cancel_input()` also sends `"stop"`. On accept, `_bind_local_seat` aliases `player/archer/combat/cover/ability` to the client's own seat replica so the camera, HUD, interaction and loot code read replicated state; the original anchor is hidden, collisionless and process-disabled until `_restore_anchor`. Input handlers branch on `_client()` and never run local combat.
- Host→client events (all `_world_*`, so an arena or delve host can never accept a world peer by name): `_world_seat` (one fighter per datagram, 20 Hz), `_world_mobs` (relevant mobs within 70 m, 15 Hz, `NetLimits` byte-budgeted batches, dead mobs at 3 Hz), `_world_arrow`/`_world_throw`/`_world_mob_arrow` (visual projectiles), `_world_loot`, `_world_kill`, `_world_reward`, `_world_waystone`, `_world_roster`/`_world_accept`/`_world_register`. Keep the state payload within the existing arena shape: like the arena, `scripts/net_limits.gd` keeps every datagram inside the 1200-byte clamp; do not add snapshot keys casually.
- `enemy.gd` and `devil_mob.gd` expose `targets` (every live ranger; `player` remains the fallback) and pick their quarry through `scripts/mob_senses.gd`, so only a visible ranger is ever acquired (see the aggression contract above), emit `attacked(attacker, amount, victim)`, and support `replica = true` with `snapshot()`/`apply_snapshot()`. Replicas are presentation-only: transform, state clock, visuals, damage numbers/blood on health deltas, and a local death that never emits `defeated` or spawns loot. The host assigns the shared ranger list every tick; `enemy.gd` also emits `arrow_fired` so bow devils' tracers replicate. `main.gd._receive_seat_attack` resolves a mob hit on a remote seat through `ArenaActor.receive_melee` (parry staggers the attacker, blocks spend seat stamina).

### Mob aggression and sight (frontier)

- `scripts/mob_senses.gd` is the single aggression model for every wilderness mob: the five devils and three forest mobs through `devil_mob.gd`, and the wolf/sentinel/cinder-devil through `enemy.gd`. Do not reintroduce a distance-only `aggro` check or a per-family copy of the cone.
- A mob is aggressive only while `MobSenses.aggressive()`: it has looked at a live ranger inside a **140° forward cone** (`HALF_FOV_DEGREES = 70`), within the profile's `sight` range (23 m legacy wolf/sentinel, 30 m cinder devil, 18-32 m per devil/forest profile), with one of two rays (chest 1.25 m, head 1.65 m) clear on mask 1 — terrain, props and tree trunks occlude; ranger and mob bodies never do. Eye height is `height * 0.85` (wolf 0.95 m body, so it looks out low).
- `MEMORY_SECONDS` is **15 s** and is the required floor: sighting refreshes it, losing sight only drains it, so a blocked line of sight never resets aggro. While memory is live and sight is not, the mob runs to `last_seen` at run speed and sweeps the ground on arrival (`_hunt`/`_devil_hunt` and the legacy CHASE branch); the old home `leash` is gone — the memory now bounds the chase, and `_return_home` reclaims the mob afterwards.
- A calm mob must not turn to face its quarry: `_face_intent` stays zero unless a state or the sighting sets it. This is what makes walking up behind a mob possible; a re-introduced unconditional `_face_intent = direction` silently destroys sneaking.
- Any landed blow names its striker through the duck-typed `note_attacker(attacker)` hook, called by `arrow.gd`, `thrown_weapon.gd`, `spell_projectile.gd`, `combat._resolve_melee_contact` and `backstab_ability.strike` before damage. Every delve projectile carries its ranger too (`delve._spawn_arrow`/`_spawn_thrown`/`_launch_spell` set `shooter`), so a shot inside the delve names its archer like one in the frontier.
- `notice()` starts an **enraged pursuit**, not a sighting memory: `hunt = HUNT_SECONDS` (90 s) tracks the quarry's live position (`focus()` returns the body while `hunt > 0`), so a mob hit from 100 m walks to the ranger through cover and never gives up at the 15 s memory. Finding the quarry (`seen`) ends the enraged hunt and hands the chase back to the memory clock. `hear()` is the weaker neighbour path: it takes the target and the alarm point but not the live track.
- `alert_pack()` reaches every `wild_mobs` member within `SHOUT_RADIUS` (12 m) through `hear_alert()`. It is raised twice: on any landed blow (`_respond_to_attack`), and **when a pursuit begins** — `MobSenses.step()` sets `woke` and calls `alert_pack` itself on the tick a mob first sights a ranger, so a pack opens the fight together instead of watching one of its own die. One hop only; the delve's `delve_devil` raises the same alarm on its first acquisition (`_wake_pack`).
- Never tick a mob every frame regardless of distance. `MobLod.slice(nearest, delta, calm)` is the only budget: `_physics_process` computes `_nearest_ranger()`, calls `_cull_presentation(nearest)`, and returns unless a slice is granted. A skipped frame accumulates into the next tick's delta, so never pass the raw frame delta past the gate. `calm` is `not _senses.aggressive()`.
- The presentation budget is applied only when `MobLod.band(nearest)` changes (cache `_present_band`): `find_children` per frame would cost more than the shadow pass it saves. Visuals expose `set_distance(distance)`; the clone's `Archer` exposes `set_shadow_casting(bool)`. Do not hide far mobs to save draws — they would pop through the thin (0.0022 density) fog.
- A calm mob is not a statue: `devil_mob`/`forest_mob` and `enemy.gd` used to `return` before the state machine when they had no target, which is why nothing ever wandered. Both now run `_calm_tick(delta)` in that case: it drives `_wander.step(self)`, `_separate()`, `_drive()`/`_drive_wolf()` and the presentation, and sets `IDLE` (inside the patch) or `RETURN` (walking home to it). Do not restore the bare early return.
- `wanders_packed()`, `wander_radius()` and `pack_family()` are the duck-typed hooks `MobWander.assign_all` needs; `assign_territory(shared, slot, group)` is the setter. Packed species merge when their spawns are within `WILD_PACK_RADIUS` (14 m, `main.gd`); a sentinel (`gargoyle`), a `gallowsroot` and every clone declare `packed: false` and never merge. `roam` is authored per profile (gargoyle 4, spawn 5, goat/warden 6, succubus/stalker 7, beast 8, wolf 10, cinder devil 12, clone 15).
- A pack fights as a group: `MobSenses.alert_packmates(struck, attacker, at, _wander.group)` ignores distance (unlike the 12 m earshot `alert_pack`), and mobs call it both in `_respond_to_attack` and when `_senses.woke` fires on a sighting, so one member seeing you is the pack seeing you.
- `move_and_slide` is the single most expensive call in a mob tick. Three rules keep it cheap and honest, and all three were measured: `max_slides = 2`; `collision_mask = 1 | 4` so a swept move never tests seventy-four other capsules; and a grounded body holds `velocity.y = 0` with `floor_stop_on_slope = false`, because pressing into the floor spent a slide of every sweep and a tilted floor cancelled horizontal motion entirely (the mobs crawled at 54% of their authored speed on any slope).
- `MobLod.move(body, delta)` is the only swept move. It scales the *horizontal* velocity by `delta / get_physics_process_delta_time()` (clamped to `DEEP_FRAMES`) so one cast covers the whole budget window, restores the body's velocity after it, and never scales the vertical component: a scaled fall drives the capsule into the floor and the recovery step eats the horizontal motion with it. A body moved this way covers exactly the same ground per second as one ticking every frame - `mob_budget_regression` fails if it does not.
- `scripts/mob_steering.gd` is the one avoidance rule for every wilderness body (devils, forest mobs, wolf, cinder devil, delve wave, clones): a 2.1 m two-height lane probe, a committed detour side held for `HOLD_SECONDS` 1.1 s, and real-travel stuck detection that flips the side and widens the probe to 2.9 m after 0.55 s below 0.30 m/s. The per-family `_steer` methods now delegate to it; do not reintroduce an ad-hoc one-ray `_steer` that cannot get around a barrier.
- The 19 m safe ring (`MobSenses.in_safe_ring`) is applied through the per-candidate `accept` filter, so a ranger inside town is never noticed, alerted, targeted or chased — the starting courts and the forest threshold trio stay decoration exactly as before.
- Senses run on the host only. Replicas skip them (the early return on `replica`) and replay the host's `state`, which already encodes pursuit.

The five species read `DevilMob.PROFILES` (one profile table), keep their imported GLB, proportions, claw cadence and authored head/strike anchors, fight bare-handed, and are scaled by `COURT_HEALTH_SCALE 0.45` / `COURT_DAMAGE_SCALE 0.65` before the same depth curve so a depth-one wave is not multiplied by four. `spawn_at` places the body and plays the emerge sigil; the snapshot carries `role`, and replicas rebuild the matching rig family (`DevilVisual` for depth roles, `DevilMobVisual` for species). `ROLE_RANGER` entries branch to `ranger_clone.gd` and go through `_clone_snapshots` instead of the devil stream.
- Cache rewards: opening the chest once pays `40 + 30*depth` gold and, on `LootRules.drops()` (10%), one deterministic item from `LOOT_TIERS` selected by depth and `run_seed + depth*7`. The host grants locally and broadcasts `_reward_event(depth,gold,item_id)`; every client grants to its own `PlayerState`. Do not double-grant on the host.
- Weapon drops: when an authoritative delve devil dies, `_on_devil_died` calls `_drop_devil_weapon`, which rolls `LootRules.drops()` (10%) before spawning a `LootPickup` at the floor under its corpse and broadcasting `_weapon_drop_event(item_id,at)`; every client spawns its own local pickup from `held_weapon_id()` (dagger→iron dagger, sword→shortsword). Pickups are armed immediately because they land away from the killer, and `_collect_loot` still banks them through each peer's `PlayerState`. Replicas drop no duplicate on `_die(false)`; only the host announces the reliable event.
- World contract: `DelveWorld.LIMIT=17.0`, `ROOM_SANCTUARY`/`ROOM_OSSUARY`, `apply_room(kind, cycle)` (idempotent rebuild), `room_title()`, `has_altar()` (sanctuary only), `palette()`, `spawn_points`, `altar_position=(0,0.05,-4.0)`, `chest_position=(0,0.06,0.0)`, `rope_position=(5.2,0.05,3.0)`, `hole_position=(-5.2,0.05,3.0)`, `hole_radius=1.05`, `drop_center=(0,8,0)`. There are **no gates**: `set_sealed` toggles additive emissive archway barriers with no colliders. `set_rope_lowered`/`set_hole_open` own rope/hatch presentation and `is_in_hole(point)` is the descent trigger. Tests address the `Rope`, `FloorHole`, `Altar` (sanctuary only), `Pentagram`, `BeaconAltar`, `BeaconRope` and `BeaconHole` nodes.
- Decision beacons (`scripts/delve_beacon.gd` + `delve_beacon.gdshader`) are additive TIME-shimmer light columns with a ground pool, a restrained OmniLight and a billboard title (`ALTAR`, `ROPE OUT`, `DESCEND`). Motion lives in shaders/tweens/particles — never per-frame GDScript — and none of it gates gameplay.

### World art and scenery (frontier and arena)

- Scenery is presentation only. `range_world.gd` terrain heights/routes/clearings, player and enemy spawns, interactables, waystones, `_reserved` gameplay clearings and village/fort collision remain authoritative. `arena_world.gd` keeps `LIMIT = 32`, the four spawn points, the 14 real cover-tree positions and their **0.55 m radius / 5.5 m height** cylinder colliders; cover and network regressions attach to those exact bodies. Art changes may replace meshes and materials, never that geometry.
- Both scenes build from `WorldStyle.frontier()` / `WorldStyle.arena()`. `TreeKit.build_variants(species, seed)` generates deterministic tapered trunks, real branches and alpha-scissored leaf/needle cards (UV2 wind weights, crown-interior vertex shading). `WorldScatter.batch` packs instances into per-chunk `MultiMesh`es with instance tints, explicit AABBs, visibility ranges and a shadow choice. `GrassField.build` places grass/flower/fern clumps with reserved/slope rejection and chunk distance culling. `WorldEnvironmentKit.build` owns the 4096 × 2048 photoreal panorama (linear-light Rayleigh gradient, sun disc authored at the key light's YXZ direction, perspective cumulus deck, cirrus and aerial haze), sky ambient, haze-matched fog, filmic grade and the shadowed sun.
- Terrain vertex colour is a value/hue multiplier; **vertex alpha is the dirt weight** consumed by `scripts/world/shaders/terrain.gdshader`, and world-normal slope selects the rock texture. Do not bake lit colour into the heightfield or bypass the splat shader for the main terrain.
- World textures are authored PNGs under `assets/world/textures/` generated by `tools/world_textures.py` (deterministic; manifest `assets/world/world_textures.json`). Keep them lossless with mipmaps and `detect_3d/compress_to=0`; alpha atlases use an 8 px inset per side. New texture ids go into `TextureLibrary` caches and, for atlases/buildings, `ATLAS_LAYOUTS`/`BUILDING_KINDS`.
- Grass and foliage use alpha scissor, not sorted transparency. Grass casts no shadows and its shader `fade_end` must equal the chunk's `visibility_range_end`; trees are chunked with shadow casting. There is no per-frame GDScript for scenery.
- Frontier tree collision follows the authored trunk (trunk height and radius), never the canopy; boulders use simple sphere colliders. Do not reintroduce the old single-cylinder/cones/spheres tree primitives or a flat-colour terrain fallback for the shipping scene.
- `tests/world_art_regression.gd` is the relevant determinism/import/budget check. It does not replace rendered inspection: launch the frontier and arena, confirm the changed sky/terrain/tree/grass surfaces in the actual camera, and report draw calls/primitives. Isolated shader-compile stalls after a teleport are not steady-state performance; sample after the scene settles.

## Cross-mode change checklist

1. Change the shared rig/combat/roll behavior first, then migrate both controllers and their callsites.
2. For new animation state, update both rig serialization methods and review actor snapshots/replica application. The host owns combat and damage; clients submit input/aim, not charge or damage claims.
3. Update the in-world kit (`world_ui.gd`), both match HUDs, the town/frontier HUD, equipment hints, and the README when controls or timing change. Never leave labels describing a removed cooldown, gesture, or launch path.
4. When gear, capacities, prices or equipped-kit behavior changes, update `PlayerState`, `EquipmentRules`, `ArmorData`, `world_ui.gd`, the town keepers/stash and both mode-start loadout paths, the network packed state, `paperdoll_regression`, `town_services_regression` and `world_ui_regression` together. Equipment is ten slots separate from the backpack; do not collapse armor to one set index or put equipped gear back in the backpack.
5. Check weapon swaps, cancellation, airborne restrictions, respawn, and host/client behavior at the affected state boundaries.
6. For world-art changes, update `WorldStyle`/`TextureLibrary`/shaders, run `world_art_regression`, and inspect rendered frontier and arena captures. Keep terrain, route, spawn, waystone and arena cover geometry unchanged unless gameplay behavior is intentionally changing.

## Restart and delivery

Godot keeps loaded scripts in memory. When asked to restart, identify processes by executable path and project arguments, preserve their scene/network arguments, close only this repo's game instances gracefully, and relaunch them. Do not kill unrelated Godot editors/games or silently force-kill a nonresponsive process. Restarting clears session-only frontier progress and disconnects a running match; reconnect clients to a restarted host.

Observe startup rather than assuming it: the title prints `IRONWOOD_MENU_READY`, the town/frontier prints `IRONWOOD_WORLD_READY`, hosts print `ARENA_HOST_READY`/`DELVE_HOST_READY`, and joined clients print `ARENA_CLIENT_READY`/`DELVE_NET_CLIENT`. Leave a user-requested game running; close temporary verification instances.

Report the exact behavior changed, exercised verification, and any remaining blocker. Update this skill when an intentional change supersedes one of its repo contracts; do not preserve obsolete aliases or old input paths just to match these notes.
